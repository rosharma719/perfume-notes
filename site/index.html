          Lift
          How many times more often notes appear together than if each were scattered independently. 1× is chance. With several notes selected, it compares a note’s share among matching perfumes to its share overall.
          Table colours
          Relative to the other notes for your selection, so busy formulas don’t light everything up. The number on a tile is the raw lift.
          Cleaning notes
          394 raw labels merged into 153: origins dropped (Jasmine (Egypt) is Jasmine), aroma-chemicals folded into the note they imitate (Galaxolide is Musk), synonyms joined. Notes from the newer sources are matched by head word and plural (Bulgarian rose is Rose); frequent ones the list lacked (honey, basil, cypress…) were added, each placed in the family it co-occurs with, with a few corrected by hand.
          De-duplicating perfumes
          Names are reduced to their distinguishing words (brand, year and concentration stripped), brand spellings that share many products are unified, and two entries merge when they have the same name in compatible years. Flankers (Intense, Elixir, Cologne) stay separate, and so do same-name perfumes with conflicting years.
          Stand-ins
          Cosine similarity of notes’ centred lift profiles. Usage-based suggestions, not odour or chemistry. Similar fragrances use note-list overlap (Jaccard).
          Data
          Notes are mapped onto a fixed list of 153. Fragrance statistics elsewhere (pairs, trios, families, trends) come from the original 26k-perfume sample; Explore, Shop and Collection use the full catalog. Ratings are Parfumo’s. Rankings ignore anything under about 40 perfumes.
          Shop
          Scores perfumes by loved notes (and notes common in your collection), your preferred families, rating, and how many notes you don’t yet own. It penalises avoided notes, near-copies of what you own, and look-alikes of what you disliked. Fit is relative to your best candidate.
