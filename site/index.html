Odd couples

Pairs that turn up together far more than their popularity predicts. Usually the two share a facet: both resins, both roasted, both green and bitter.

Stand-ins

Notes that keep the same company but rarely keep each other’s. They fill the same slot in a formula, so one can often cover for the other when you’re out of stock, over budget or restricted. The score is how alike their partner lists are.

How this was made

Lift is how many times more often two notes appear together than if each were scattered independently. 1× is chance, 3× is three times as often. With several notes selected, it compares a note’s share among matching perfumes with its share overall.

Cleaning. 394 raw labels were merged into 153. Origins were dropped (Jasmine (Egypt) becomes Jasmine), aroma-chemicals folded into the note they imitate (Galaxolide (Musk) becomes Musk), and synonyms joined. Families are hand-assigned.

Stand-ins compare each note’s lift against every other note, centred so that only distinctive preferences count, then take the cosine similarity. They are suggestions from usage data, not a statement about odour or chemistry.

Source. The open doevent/perfume dataset, a 2024 snapshot, not a direct Fragrantica export. Note lists are flat, with no top, heart or base. Mass-market ranges are heavily represented, so “common” leans clean, sweet and musky. Tables ignore anything under about 40 perfumes.
